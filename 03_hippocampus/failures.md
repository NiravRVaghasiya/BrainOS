# ❌ Hippocampus — Failures & Disorders

## Anterograde Amnesia (Can't Form New Memories)

### Cause: Hippocampal damage (bilateral)
- **Result**: Can't encode new declarative memories
- **Experience**: Living in a permanent present moment
- **Preserved**: Personality, intelligence, old memories, procedural learning
- **Devastating because**: New experiences vanish within minutes

### Famous Cases:
- **Patient H.M.**: Surgical removal → 55 years of anterograde amnesia (1953-2008)
- **Clive Wearing**: Viral encephalitis destroyed hippocampus → "wakes up" every 30 seconds
- **Korsakoff's Syndrome**: Alcohol-related thiamine deficiency → hippocampal necrosis

## Retrograde Amnesia (Losing Old Memories)

### Temporally Graded (Ribot's Law):
- Most recent memories lost first → oldest preserved longest
- Why: Recent memories still hippocampus-dependent; old ones already in cortex
- Time window: Typically last 1-10 years are vulnerable

### Flat (Non-Graded):
- ALL autobiographical memories lost regardless of age
- Suggests extensive cortical damage too (not just hippocampus)
- Rare; usually from severe TBI or extensive anoxia

## Alzheimer's Disease

### Hippocampal Involvement:
- Hippocampus is one of the FIRST structures affected
- Neurofibrillary tangles + amyloid plaques accumulate here early
- Explains why memory loss is the first symptom
- Progresses: Hippocampus → Entorhinal Cortex → Temporal Cortex → Widespread

### Timeline:
1. Mild: Forgetting recent conversations, misplacing objects, repetition
2. Moderate: Can't form new memories, old memories degrade, disorientation
3. Severe: Cannot recognize family, complete loss of autobiographical identity

## PTSD (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder)

### Hippocampal Dysfunction:
- Chronic cortisol exposure → hippocampal ATROPHY (up to 10% volume loss)
- Pattern separation FAILS → safe contexts trigger trauma responses (overgeneralization)
- Context-dependent memory FAILS → trauma memories intrude without appropriate context
- Can't tag memories as "past" → experienced as happening NOW (flashbacks)

## Transient Global Amnesia (TGA)
- **What**: Sudden onset of anterograde amnesia lasting 1-24 hours
- **Cause**: Temporary disruption of hippocampal blood flow
- **Experience**: Repeatedly asking "Where am I?" "What day is it?" every 2 minutes
- **Recovery**: Complete (no lasting damage). Terrifying but benign.

## Age-Related Hippocampal Decline
- **Volume loss**: ~1-2% per year after age 50
- **Neurogenesis**: Drops significantly (fewer new dentate gyrus neurons)
- **Pattern separation**: Weakens → more source confusion ("Did I read that or dream it?")
- **Consolidation**: Slower → need more repetitions and more sleep
- **BUT**: Exercise can partially reverse decline (increases BDNF → neurogenesis)

## Epilepsy (Temporal Lobe)
- **Problem**: Hippocampal sclerosis (scarring) from repeated seizures
- **Result**: Abnormal electrical activity disrupts encoding/retrieval
- **Treatment**: Sometimes surgical removal (→ planned memory deficits vs. uncontrolled seizures)
- **Irony**: Patient H.M.'s surgery CURED his epilepsy but created the most famous amnesia case in history

## Stress and the Hippocampus
| Stress Level | Duration | Effect on Hippocampus |
|-------------|----------|----------------------|
| Acute moderate | Minutes | Enhanced encoding (norepinephrine boost) |
| Acute severe | Hours | Impaired encoding (cortisol floods receptors) |
| Chronic | Weeks-Months | Structural damage (dendritic retraction, reduced neurogenesis) |
| Severe/prolonged | Years | Volume loss (5-10%), impaired consolidation |
