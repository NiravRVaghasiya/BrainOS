# 🔬 Sensory Buffer — Real-World Examples

## Iconic (Visual) Memory in Action

### 1. Sperling's Partial Report (1960)
- Flash a 3×4 grid of letters for 50ms
- Participants can only report ~4-5 letters (seems like they saw few)
- BUT: If you cue a row IMMEDIATELY with a tone → they get all 3-4 in that row
- **Proves**: They SAW all 12 letters but the image faded before they could report them all

### 2. Change Blindness
- You don't notice a gorilla walking through a basketball game (Simons & Chabris, 1999)
- Person you're talking to gets swapped behind a door → you don't notice
- **Why**: Unattended items in the buffer are never encoded. You think you see them, but you don't.

### 3. Movie Continuity Errors
- Actors hold coffee cups that switch hands between cuts
- You never notice because each frame refreshes your iconic buffer from scratch
- Your brain assumes continuity rather than checking

## Echoic Memory in Action

### 4. The "What?" Effect
- Someone says something. You say "What?" Then realize you DID hear them.
- **Why**: Echoic buffer held the sound for ~3s. Your belated attention accessed it.

### 5. Cocktail Party Effect
- You're ignoring a conversation nearby. Then they say YOUR NAME.
- Your name "pops" into awareness from the echoic buffer (it was being processed subconsciously)
- Proves: Unattended audio IS processed to the level of personal significance

### 6. Sentence Parsing
- "Time flies like an arrow; fruit flies like a banana."
- You need the FULL sentence buffered to parse the pun — echoic memory provides this.

## Haptic Memory in Action

### 7. Phantom Phone Vibrations
- You "feel" your phone vibrate when it didn't
- Primed haptic system generates false positives from habituated sensations
- The 2s haptic buffer creates a "still feeling it" aftereffect

### 8. Walking in the Dark
- You touch a wall, then step away. For ~2 seconds you still "feel" where it was.
- Haptic buffer maintains spatial touch information for navigation

## Cross-Modal

### 9. The McGurk Effect
- Hear "ba" + see lips saying "fa" → perceive "da"
- Proves sensory buffers don't just record — they integrate across modalities in real-time
