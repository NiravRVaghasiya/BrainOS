# ⚙️ Motor Memory — Mechanisms

## The Motor Learning Circuit

```
    CORTEX (Motor Plan)
        ↓
    BASAL GANGLIA               CEREBELLUM
    (Action selection           (Error correction
     & habit formation)          & timing calibration)
        ↓                            ↓
    THALAMUS                    THALAMUS
        ↓                            ↓
    PRIMARY MOTOR CORTEX (M1) → Spinal cord → Muscles
```

## Basal Ganglia: Habit Formation Machine

### The Striatal Learning Loop:
1. **Cortex** sends intended action to Striatum (input station)
2. **Dopamine signal**: Was outcome better or worse than expected?
   - Better → Dopamine burst → STRENGTHEN this action-selection
   - Worse → Dopamine dip → WEAKEN this action-selection
3. Over 1000s of repetitions: Correct sequence becomes automatic
4. Transfers from "goal-directed" (deliberate) to "habitual" (automatic)

### Direct vs. Indirect Pathway:
- **Direct pathway** (Go!): Facilitates the chosen action
- **Indirect pathway** (Stop!): Suppresses competing actions
- Balanced = smooth, decisive movement
- Imbalanced = Parkinson's (can't initiate) or Huntington's (can't suppress)

## Cerebellum: The Error Corrector

### The Climbing Fiber Teaching Signal:
1. You attempt a movement (e.g., reach for a cup)
2. **Predicted outcome** (internal model) vs. **Actual outcome** (sensory feedback)
3. **If mismatch**: Climbing fiber from Inferior Olive fires → error signal
4. **Purkinje cells** adjust their firing → internal model updates
5. Next attempt is more accurate
6. After 100s of trials: Error ≈ 0 → movement is calibrated

### Internal Forward Models:
- Cerebellum builds a SIMULATION of your body
- Predicts sensory consequences of each movement BEFORE feedback arrives
- This is why you can catch a ball: You predict where your hand will be
- Damage → dysmetria (constant overshooting/undershooting)

## Three Stages of Motor Learning

### Stage 1: Cognitive (Prefrontal-dependent)
- Slow, deliberate, attention-heavy, error-prone
- PFC + Cerebellum actively engaged
- Verbal instructions guide movement ("elbow up, step forward")
- **Duration**: First few sessions

### Stage 2: Associative (Striatal consolidation)
- Fewer errors, less attention required
- Basal ganglia takes over action selection
- Chunks of movement become linked
- **Duration**: Days to weeks

### Stage 3: Autonomous (Cerebellar + Basal Ganglia only)
- Fast, effortless, attention-free, parallel processing
- Cortex barely involved (frees it for other tasks)
- Disrupted by conscious attention ("centipede effect")
- **Duration**: Permanent once reached

## Sleep Consolidation of Motor Memories
- Motor sequences IMPROVE after sleep without additional practice
- Spindle activity (12-15 Hz) during NREM Stage 2 correlates with motor improvement
- The brain optimizes: Removes unnecessary co-contractions, smooths transitions
- "Sleep on it" works for physical skills, not just intellectual ones
