# ❌ Sensory Buffer — Failures & Disorders

## Normal "Failures" (By Design)

### Inattentional Blindness
- **What**: Failing to see clearly visible things because attention is elsewhere
- **Example**: Radiologists miss a gorilla image inserted into CT scans (83% miss rate!)
- **Mechanism**: Sensory buffer CAPTURES the image but attention gate REJECTS it
- **Not a deficiency**: It's a feature — unlimited sensory input, limited processing capacity

### Change Blindness
- **What**: Failure to detect large changes in visual scenes across interruptions
- **Example**: 50% of people don't notice when the person they're giving directions to is swapped for someone else (during a brief occlusion)
- **Mechanism**: Buffer refreshes on each new fixation; without explicit attention to a feature, changes don't register

## Pathological Failures

### Visual Agnosia (Apperceptive type)
- **What**: Sensory buffer intact, but can't integrate features into coherent percepts
- **Cause**: Damage to visual association areas (V2-V4)
- **Result**: Can see color, motion, edges — but can't recognize objects
- **Difference from blindness**: Eyes work fine, V1 responds, but percept construction fails

### Auditory Processing Disorder (APD)
- **What**: Normal hearing but impaired buffering/parsing of speech sounds
- **Cause**: Abnormal temporal processing in auditory cortex
- **Result**: Echoic buffer too short or noisy → "I can hear you but can't understand"
- **Prevalence**: 2-7% of children

### Sensory Overload (Autism Spectrum)
- **What**: Reduced thalamic gating → TOO MUCH passes through the attention filter
- **Mechanism**: Weak GABAergic inhibition at sensory gates
- **Result**: Overwhelming sensory input that can't be filtered → distress, shutdown
- **Opposite of inattentional blindness**: Everything gets through, nothing is suppressed

### Hemispatial Neglect
- **What**: Brain damage (usually right parietal) → left visual field is "deleted" from awareness
- **Paradox**: Sensory buffer captures BOTH sides, but attention unilaterally fails
- **Evidence**: Patients can unconsciously process neglected stimuli (priming effects from unseen side)

## Age-Related Changes
- **Iconic memory duration**: Slightly shorter in elderly (~150ms vs ~250ms)
- **Attentional narrowing**: Older adults have smaller effective spotlight
- **Echoic persistence**: Generally preserved (good news for hearing comprehension)
- **Speed of transfer**: Slower extraction from buffer → more is lost before encoding

## Drug Effects
| Substance | Effect on Sensory Buffer |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| Caffeine | Slightly faster attentional extraction |
| Alcohol | Reduced buffer capacity, slower gating |
| Psychedelics | Disrupted gating → sensory flooding, synesthesia |
| Benzodiazepines | Enhanced GABA → stronger suppression → "tunnel vision" |
