# 🔬 Motor Memory — Real-World Examples

## Automaticity in Action

### 1. Typing
- Experts type 60-120 WPM without looking or thinking about key positions
- Ask a typist WHERE the 'G' key is → they have to mime typing to figure it out
- The knowledge is in the MOTOR SYSTEM, not declarative memory
- Proves: Procedural and declarative are independent systems

### 2. Riding a Bicycle (The Classic)
- Learn once → retain for decades without practice
- After 20 years: First few seconds wobbly, then fully automatic within 30 seconds
- The motor program is stored in cerebellum + basal ganglia (not hippocampus)
- This is why amnesics can still ride bikes (H.M. learned mirror tracing!)

### 3. Catching a Ball
- Ball thrown → brain calculates trajectory in ~200ms (cerebellar forward model)
- Hand moves to predicted intercept point BEFORE the ball arrives
- You can't verbally explain the physics — your cerebellum just "knows"
- Practice makes the forward model more accurate (athletes > novices)

## The Automaticity Trap

### 4. The "Centipede Effect"
- A centipede walks perfectly until someone asks "which leg goes first?"
- Then it can't walk at all
- Conscious attention to automated skills DISRUPTS them
- Sports: "Choking" under pressure = prefrontal interfering with basal ganglia automaticity

### 5. Expert Reversal Difficulty
- Touch typists asked to type on a QWERTY layout with letters rearranged
- They're SLOWER than hunt-and-peck typists to adapt
- Strong motor programs resist modification (the old program keeps triggering)
- Relates to: Why changing a golf swing is so hard for experts

## Offline Improvement

### 6. Piano Practice + Sleep
- Practice a finger sequence (4-1-3-2-4) until plateau
- Sleep for 8 hours: Performance IMPROVES 20% without additional practice
- Stay awake 8 hours: No improvement (or slight degradation)
- Stage 2 NREM spindles correlate with overnight motor learning gains

### 7. Athletic Performance Gains
- Marathon runners report "breakthrough runs" after rest days
- The body doesn't improve during training — it improves during RECOVERY
- Neural: Motor programs optimized offline (cerebellar consolidation)
- This is why overtraining backfires (no consolidation time)

## Motor Memory's Independence from Declarative

### 8. Amnesic Patients Learning Tasks (Milner, 1962)
- Patient H.M.: Learned mirror tracing across days (performance improved each session)
- But each day: "I've never done this before" (no EPISODIC memory of learning)
- Performance improved without any conscious memory of practice
- Definitive proof: Motor learning ≠ declarative learning

### 9. Musical Instrument Mastery
- Concert pianists play pieces with 10+ notes per second per hand
- Impossible to consciously plan each movement — it's fully cerebellar
- The "10,000 hour rule" is really: ~10,000 hours to achieve full automaticity
- Stage 3 (autonomous) playing → frees cortex for EXPRESSION and INTERPRETATION

### 10. Language Articulation
- Speaking requires 100+ muscles coordinated within milliseconds
- Children take ~5 years to fully automatize speech motor programs
- Second language accent: Motor programs for L1 sounds interfere with L2 (competing motor maps)
- Complete accent elimination requires MOTOR retraining, not just knowledge
