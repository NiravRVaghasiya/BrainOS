# ❌ Working Memory — Failures & Disorders

## Normal Limitations

### Distraction-Induced Forgetting
- **What**: A single interruption wipes WM contents
- **Example**: Walk into a room → forget why you came (doorway effect)
- **Mechanism**: Context change triggers gate-opening, which flushes current contents
- **The doorway effect**: Spatial context boundaries trigger WM reset (Radvansky, 2011)

### Attentional Blink
- **What**: Miss a second target if it appears 200-500ms after the first
- **Mechanism**: Consolidating item 1 into WM temporarily closes the gate
- **Duration**: ~200-500ms window of vulnerability

### Choking Under Pressure
- **What**: Performance drops when stakes are high
- **Mechanism**: Anxiety thoughts consume WM slots → less capacity for the task
- **Example**: Math test anxiety → rumination uses phonological loop → can't do calculations
- **Ironic**: The more you try NOT to worry, the more WM it consumes (ironic process theory)

## Clinical Disorders

### ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder)
- **WM deficit**: Central executive dysfunction — can't maintain goals, filter distractions
- **Mechanism**: Dopamine dysregulation in prefrontal circuits → gate too loose
- **Impact**: Can't hold multi-step instructions, loses track mid-task
- **Working memory training**: Shows some improvement but limited transfer

### Schizophrenia
- **WM deficit**: Severe DLPFC hypofrontality → can't maintain items in mind
- **Mechanism**: Dopamine imbalance + GABA interneuron dysfunction in PFC
- **Impact**: Disorganized speech (loses thread), can't plan ahead, poverty of thought
- **One of the strongest cognitive markers** (even in unaffected relatives)

### Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)
- **WM deficit**: Prefrontal cortex is most vulnerable to impact damage
- **Common complaint**: "I can't keep track of things anymore"
- **Recovery**: Partial, depends on severity and rehabilitation

### Aging (Normal Cognitive Decline)
- **What declines**: Central executive (updating, inhibition) — NOT phonological store
- **Why**: Prefrontal cortex atrophies first (2% volume loss per decade after 50)
- **Intact**: Verbal short-term span (digit span) holds up well into 70s
- **Impaired**: Complex span tasks (doing two things at once) declines significantly

### Intellectual Disability
- **WM capacity**: Strongly correlated with IQ (r ≈ 0.7)
- **Mechanistically**: Fewer PFC neurons, less efficient theta-gamma coupling
- **Training**: Can improve WM scores but doesn't raise IQ (limited transfer)

## Pharmacological Disruption

| Agent | Effect on WM | Mechanism |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| Alcohol | ↓↓ Severe impairment | GABA enhancement → PFC suppression |
| Cannabis (THC) | ↓ Moderate impairment | Disrupts hippocampal-PFC theta coupling |
| Caffeine | ↑ Slight improvement | Dopamine/norepinephrine boost in PFC |
| Methylphenidate (Ritalin) | ↑↑ Significant improvement | Optimizes dopaminergic gating in PFC |
| Benzodiazepines | ↓↓↓ Severe | GABAergic suppression of all PFC activity |
| Sleep deprivation (24h) | ↓↓ ~30-40% reduction | PFC metabolic shutdown (no glucose) |

## The WM Training Debate
- **Claim**: "Brain training games" improve WM capacity
- **Evidence**: Training improves performance ON THAT SPECIFIC TASK
- **Transfer**: Very limited transfer to untrained tasks or real-world outcomes
- **Consensus (2024)**: WM capacity is largely hardware-limited; training optimizes USE of existing capacity, doesn't expand the hardware
