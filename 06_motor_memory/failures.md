# ❌ Motor Memory — Failures & Disorders

## Basal Ganglia Disorders

### Parkinson's Disease
- **Problem**: Dopaminergic neurons in Substantia Nigra die
- **Result**: Can't initiate or smooth voluntary movements
- **Motor memory impact**: 
  - Existing automated skills become inaccessible (freezing)
  - New motor learning severely impaired
  - Sequence learning particularly affected
- **Paradox**: Can sometimes move in emergencies (cortex bypasses basal ganglia)

### Huntington's Disease
- **Problem**: Medium spiny neurons in Striatum degenerate
- **Result**: Involuntary movements (chorea) + can't suppress unwanted actions
- **Motor memory impact**: Can't form new habits, existing motor programs corrupted
- **Cognitive**: Also affects cognitive habits and procedural reasoning

### Tourette Syndrome
- **Problem**: Basal ganglia hyperactivity → motor programs fire involuntarily
- **Result**: Tics (brief, repetitive, stereotyped movements/vocalizations)
- **Mechanism**: Motor programs with too-low activation thresholds
- **Treatment**: Habit reversal training (competing response → new motor program)

## Cerebellar Disorders

### Cerebellar Ataxia
- **Problem**: Purkinje cell degeneration → error correction fails
- **Result**: Movements are inaccurate, poorly timed, decomposed
- **Dysmetria**: Overshooting/undershooting targets
- **Dysdiadochokinesia**: Can't perform rapid alternating movements
- **Motor learning**: Almost completely abolished (can't refine through practice)

### Cerebellar Cognitive Affective Syndrome
- **Discovery**: Cerebellum also contributes to cognition and emotion (not just motor)
- **Result**: Planning difficulties, language issues, emotional dysregulation
- **Reframes**: Cerebellum is a general-purpose "smoother" for ALL brain operations

## Apraxia (Motor Memory Access Failure)

### Ideomotor Apraxia
- **What**: Can't perform skilled movements on command DESPITE intact motor system
- **"Show me how you use a hammer"** → Bizarre, incorrect movements
- **BUT**: Can use a hammer normally when actually given one (context triggers the program)
- **Location**: Left parietal damage (where motor plans are STORED, not executed)
- **Key insight**: The motor program exists but can't be voluntarily ACCESSED

### Ideational Apraxia
- **What**: Can perform individual actions but can't SEQUENCE them correctly
- **"Make a cup of tea"** → Puts teabag in cup, then tries to pour from empty kettle
- **Problem**: The executive sequencing of motor subroutines is lost
- **Location**: Frontal-parietal disconnection

## Dystonia
- **What**: Sustained involuntary muscle contractions → abnormal postures
- **Musician's dystonia**: Focal dystonia in over-practiced musicians (pianist's hands curl)
- **Mechanism**: Motor maps in cortex OVER-consolidate → neighboring representations merge
- **Ironic**: TOO MUCH practice without rest → aberrant plasticity
- **Treatment**: Sensory retraining, botox (weakens muscle), constraint therapy

## Age-Related Motor Decline
| Aspect | Change | Mechanism |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Speed | Slower (20-40% by age 70) | Reduced dopamine, demyelination |
| Accuracy | Slightly reduced | Cerebellar Purkinje cell loss |
| New learning | Slower but possible | Reduced plasticity, fewer dopamine neurons |
| Existing skills | Well preserved into 80s+ | Deeply consolidated, widely distributed |
| Balance | Significantly impaired | Cerebellar atrophy + proprioceptive loss |

## The Use-It-or-Lose-It Principle for Motor Memory
- Motor programs CAN decay with extreme disuse (years without practice)
- But they're remarkably persistent: 90% retained after 10 years
- Relearning is always faster than initial learning (savings effect)
- The "scaffold" remains even when performance degrades
