# ❌ Language Networks — Failures & Disorders

## Aphasias (Language Loss After Brain Damage)

### Broca's Aphasia (Expressive/Non-fluent)
- **Location**: Left inferior frontal gyrus (BA 44/45)
- **Speech**: Telegraphic, effortful, agrammatic ("Man... dog... walk")
- **Comprehension**: Largely preserved (but fails for complex syntax)
- **Writing**: Similarly impaired
- **Awareness**: HIGH (patient is frustrated — knows they can't speak properly)
- **Recovery**: Partial; intensive speech therapy helps

### Wernicke's Aphasia (Receptive/Fluent)
- **Location**: Left posterior superior temporal gyrus (BA 22)
- **Speech**: Fluent, grammatical, but meaningless (jargon, neologisms)
- **Comprehension**: Severely impaired (can't understand spoken or written language)
- **Writing**: Fluent but nonsensical
- **Awareness**: LOW (patient doesn't realize they're not making sense — anosognosia)
- **Prognosis**: Often poorer than Broca's

### Conduction Aphasia
- **Location**: Arcuate fasciculus (connecting Broca's and Wernicke's)
- **Key deficit**: Cannot REPEAT what they just heard (despite understanding it)
- **Speech**: Relatively fluent with word-finding pauses
- **Comprehension**: Intact
- **Proves**: Repetition requires sound-to-motor MAPPING (dorsal stream)

### Global Aphasia
- **Location**: Massive left hemisphere damage (MCA stroke)
- **Result**: All language functions devastated
- **Can often**: Produce automatic speech (counting, swearing, song lyrics)
- **Proves**: Automatic/emotional language uses different pathways (right hemisphere, basal ganglia)

## Developmental Language Disorders

### Specific Language Impairment (SLI / DLD)
- **What**: Language significantly below age norms despite normal intelligence
- **Prevalence**: ~7% of children
- **Features**: Grammar particularly affected (verb tenses, complex sentences)
- **Mechanism**: Possible procedural memory deficit (basal ganglia) → can't extract grammatical rules
- **Outcome**: Persists into adulthood; compensatory strategies develop

### Dyslexia
- **What**: Reading difficulty despite normal intelligence and instruction
- **Core deficit**: Phonological processing (mapping sounds to letters)
- **Memory impact**: Phonological loop impaired → can't hold sound patterns for decoding
- **Brain difference**: Reduced activation in left temporo-parietal regions during reading
- **NOT "seeing letters backward"** — that's a myth

## Neurodegenerative Language Loss

### Primary Progressive Aphasia (PPA)
- **What**: Gradual language deterioration (years) as first symptom of neurodegeneration
- **Semantic variant**: Loses word meanings first ("What IS a stapler?")
- **Non-fluent variant**: Loses grammar/fluency first (like progressive Broca's)
- **Logopenic variant**: Word-finding gets progressively worse
- **Devastating**: Language is often last thing people expect to lose

### Alzheimer's — Language Timeline
1. **Early**: Word-finding difficulties, circumlocution ("the thing you write with")
2. **Middle**: Simplified sentences, repetition, naming failures
3. **Late**: Echolalia (parrot-like repetition), eventual mutism
4. **Preserved longest**: Procedural language (greetings, automatic phrases, swearing)

## Acquired Reading Disorders

### Surface Dyslexia (Regularization)
- Reads "yacht" as "yatched" (applies phonological rules to irregular words)
- Semantic route (whole-word recognition) is damaged
- Falls back on letter-by-letter sounding out
- Brain: Temporal lobe damage (where whole-word forms are stored)

### Deep Dyslexia (Semantic Errors)
- Reads "ship" as "boat" (activates meaning but picks wrong word)
- Reads "sympathy" as "orchestra" (bizarre semantic leaps)
- Phonological route completely destroyed; only meaning route remains
- Evidence for dual-route model of reading

## The Resilience of Language Memory
- Even in severe Alzheimer's: Familiar songs' LYRICS are often preserved
- Even in global aphasia: Swear words, counting, and singing remain
- Procedural language memory (automated phrases) is the LAST to go
- Shows: Deeply over-learned language becomes motor/procedural (basal ganglia, not cortex)
