# 🔬 Hippocampus — Real-World Examples

## Pattern Separation in Action

### 1. "Where Did I Park TODAY?"
- You park in the same garage every day, different spots
- Hippocampus must create DISTINCT traces for each day's spot
- When it works: "Today I'm in B4" (separate from yesterday's C7)
- When it fails: "Wait... was that today or yesterday?" (Interference)

### 2. Similar-Looking People
- Your brain creating distinct representations for twins or look-alikes
- New employee who resembles your college friend → hippocampus separates them
- If pattern separation is weak → you merge their histories in memory

## Pattern Completion in Action

### 3. Proust's Madeleine
- Tasting a madeleine cookie → involuntary flood of childhood memories
- Single sensory cue (taste) → CA3 completes the entire autobiographical episode
- Proves: Memories are stored as distributed patterns; ANY piece can retrieve the whole

### 4. Song Recognition
- Hear the first 3 notes of a song → immediately know the entire melody + lyrics + where you first heard it
- Even hearing it in a different key works (pattern completion is abstracted)

### 5. Déjà Vu
- Partial match between current situation and stored memory
- Pattern completion fires inappropriately → "I've been here before" feeling
- But no full episodic retrieval → eerie familiarity without specific recall

## Consolidation Evidence

### 6. Patient H.M. (Henry Molaison, 1926-2008)
- 1953: Bilateral hippocampal removal for epilepsy
- **Immediate result**: Could NOT form new declarative memories (lived in 30-second windows)
- **Preserved**: Old memories from years before surgery (already consolidated to cortex)
- **Also preserved**: Could learn new motor skills (mirror tracing) — different system!
- **Legacy**: Proved hippocampus = gateway, not warehouse

### 7. Temporally Graded Retrograde Amnesia (Ribot's Law)
- Hippocampal damage → recent memories lost, remote memories preserved
- The "gradient": Last few years wiped, childhood intact
- Proves: Consolidation transfers memories from hippocampus to cortex over time
- Fully consolidated memories don't need the hippocampus anymore

### 8. Sleep and Exam Performance
- Students who sleep 8 hours after studying: 20-30% better recall next day
- Students who pull all-nighters: Information never consolidates properly
- The hippocampus replays study material during deep sleep → cortex stores it

## Place Cells / Cognitive Maps

### 9. London Taxi Drivers (Maguire et al., 2000)
- London cabbies have LARGER posterior hippocampi than bus drivers
- More years driving → larger hippocampus
- The brain literally GREW more tissue to store the complex spatial map
- Navigation expertise = hippocampal structural plasticity in action

### 10. GPS and Hippocampal Atrophy
- Heavy GPS users show REDUCED hippocampal activation during navigation
- Concern: "Use it or lose it" — outsourcing navigation may weaken spatial memory
- Contrast: People who navigate by memory maintain hippocampal volume

## Replay During Rest

### 11. Rat Maze Replay (Wilson & McNaughton, 1994)
- Recorded hippocampal place cells during maze running AND during sleep after
- Same firing sequences replayed during sleep — IN THE CORRECT ORDER
- Compressed to ~20x speed
- Replay correlates with later memory performance (more replay = better memory)
