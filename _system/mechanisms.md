# ⚙️ System Infrastructure — Core Mechanisms

## The Four Pillars of Memory Infrastructure

### 1. Energy Supply (Glucose + Oxygen)
- Brain: 2% of body weight, 20% of energy consumption
- Neurons can't store energy — need constant blood supply
- Any interruption (stroke, anoxia) → cell death within 4-6 minutes
- Glucose is the PRIMARY fuel (ketones as backup during fasting)

### 2. Myelin (Signal Speed)
- White matter sheaths around axons: Insulation for electrical transmission
- Increases signal speed from 2 m/s → 120 m/s (60x faster)
- Continues developing until age ~25 (frontal lobe last)
- Demyelination (MS) → signal degradation → cognitive/motor symptoms

### 3. Blood-Brain Barrier (Protection)
- Tight junctions between endothelial cells
- Protects neurons from toxins, immune cells, pathogens
- Selective transport: Glucose in, waste out
- Drugs must be designed to cross it (challenge for brain medications)

### 4. Glial Cells (Support Infrastructure)
- **Astrocytes**: Metabolic support, synapse formation, neurotransmitter recycling
- **Oligodendrocytes**: Produce myelin sheaths
- **Microglia**: Immune defense, synaptic pruning (removing weak connections!)
- **Outnumber neurons**: ~170 billion glia vs ~86 billion neurons
- Recent discovery: Glia actively participate in information processing (not just support)

## The Glymphatic System (Brain's Waste Disposal)

### Discovery (2012, Nedergaard Lab):
- During SLEEP: Brain shrinks by 60% (interstitial space expands)
- Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) flushes through expanded channels
- Removes metabolic waste: Amyloid-beta, tau, other toxins
- 10x more active during sleep than wakefulness

### Implications for Memory:
- Sleep deprivation → waste accumulation → neurotoxicity
- Chronic sleep loss → Alzheimer's risk (amyloid not cleared)
- DEEP SLEEP (slow-wave) is when glymphatic clearance peaks
- This is another reason why sleep is non-negotiable for brain health

## Homeostatic Plasticity (Keeping the System Balanced)

### Synaptic Scaling:
- If a neuron gets TOO MUCH input → scales DOWN all synapses proportionally
- If a neuron gets TOO LITTLE input → scales UP all synapses
- Maintains useful signal-to-noise ratio
- Happens during sleep: Global synaptic downscaling (SHY hypothesis — Tononi & Cirelli)

### The Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis (SHY):
1. **Waking**: Learning strengthens synapses (LTP) → total synaptic weight INCREASES
2. **Problem**: If this continued indefinitely → saturation → can't learn anything new
3. **Sleep (SWS)**: Global downscaling — ALL synapses weakened proportionally
4. **Result**: Strong memories survive (above threshold), weak ones pruned
5. **Morning**: Brain is "refreshed" — room to learn again
6. **This is why you feel mentally "full" after an intense learning day**
