# 🔬 Emotional Tagging — Real-World Examples

## Enhanced Encoding

### 1. Flashbulb Memories
- "Where were you on 9/11?" → Most adults have vivid, detailed recall
- The emotion TAGS the entire context: location, who you were with, what you were doing
- Confidence is very high... but accuracy is only moderate (Talarico & Rubin, 2003)
- Proves: Emotion boosts VIVIDNESS and CONFIDENCE more than ACCURACY

### 2. First Kiss / First Heartbreak
- Emotionally charged "firsts" are remembered with extraordinary detail
- The combination of novelty + high emotional arousal = maximum encoding
- You remember the shirt color, the weather, the exact words — or think you do

### 3. Trauma Encoding (Too Much Emotion)
- Soldier remembers the sound of an explosion with excruciating clarity
- BUT: Can't remember what happened immediately before or after (peritraumatic amnesia)
- Extreme amygdala activation: Central details enhanced, peripheral details SUPPRESSED
- This is "weapon focus": Victims remember the gun but not the face

## Emotional Priority in Retrieval

### 4. Mood-Congruent Memory
- Depressed people disproportionately recall negative memories
- Happy people disproportionately recall positive memories
- Current emotional state acts as a RETRIEVAL CUE for matching memories
- Creates a self-reinforcing loop (depression → negative memories → more depression)

### 5. Fear Conditioning (One-Trial Learning)
- Touch hot stove ONCE → lifetime avoidance
- Dog bites child ONCE → lasting dog phobia
- The amygdala creates a permanent stimulus-response link
- Evolutionarily critical: Can't afford to need multiple trials for survival threats

### 6. The "Reminiscence Bump" (Ages 15-25)
- Elderly adults recall MORE memories from their teens/early 20s than other periods
- Why: This period has the most novel + emotionally intense experiences
- First love, first job, leaving home, identity formation — all heavily tagged

## Emotion and Forgetting

### 7. Directed Forgetting Fails for Emotional Items
- "Forget the previous list" → works for neutral words
- Emotional words RESIST directed forgetting
- Amygdala tagging makes items harder to suppress/inhibit
- Clinical relevance: Why PTSD patients can't "just stop thinking about it"

### 8. Sleep and Emotional Detox
- Experience a scary event → Sleep → Next day: Remember the WHAT but feel less of the HOW
- REM sleep: Replays emotional memories WITHOUT norepinephrine (decouples content from arousal)
- Adaptive: You learn from emotional events without being perpetually activated
- When this fails: PTSD (nightmares replay WITH full emotional activation → no extinction)

## Reward-Based Emotional Tagging

### 9. The Surprise Bonus
- Get an unexpected $20 tip → remember the whole encounter vividly
- Dopamine prediction error → hippocampal enhancement
- Everything surrounding the unexpected reward gets encoded (context, person, location)
- This is why surprise gifts are remembered better than expected ones

### 10. Music and Emotional Memory
- Hearing "your song" → entire relationship history floods back
- Music activates amygdala + hippocampus simultaneously (PET studies)
- Even Alzheimer's patients who can't form new memories respond to their era's music
- Music is multiply encoded: Sound + Emotion + Motor + Autobiographical context
